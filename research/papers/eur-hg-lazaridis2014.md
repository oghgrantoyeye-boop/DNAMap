# Lazaridis et al. 2014 — three ancestral populations for present-day Europeans

- **Citation:** Lazaridis I, Patterson N, Mittnik A, et al. (2014) Ancient human genomes suggest three ancestral populations for present-day Europeans. *Nature* 513:409–413.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/nature13673 · PMC4170574 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. Genomes of a ~7,000-year-old farmer from Germany (Stuttgart, LBK) and ~8,000-year-old hunter-gatherers from Luxembourg (Loschbour) and Sweden (Motala). *(Abstract.)*
2. Most present-day Europeans can be modelled with at least three deeply differentiated ancestral sources: West European Hunter-Gatherers (WHG), Ancient North Eurasians (ANE, related to Upper Palaeolithic Siberians), and Early European Farmers (EEF, mainly of Near Eastern origin with some WHG-related ancestry). *(Abstract.)*
3. EEF are modelled with ~44% ancestry from a "Basal Eurasian" lineage that split before other non-African lineages diversified. *(Abstract; admixture-graph section.)*

## Notes
- "Basal Eurasian" is a modelled ghost lineage (no sampled individuals) — must be shown as inferred, not as a mapped population.
