# Schlebusch et al. 2017 — southern African ancient genomes

- **Citation:** Schlebusch CM, Malmström H, Günther T, et al. (2017) Southern African ancient genomes estimate modern human divergence to 350,000 to 260,000 years ago. *Science* 358:652–655.
- **DOI:** 10.1126/science.aao6266 · **Verification:** abstract_only

## Claims we use (abstract only)
1. Three ~2000-year-old Stone Age hunter-gatherers from KwaZulu-Natal resemble present-day southern San; four Iron Age farmers (300–500 years old) resemble present-day Bantu-language speakers.
2. Present-day Khoe-San groups carry 9–30% admixture from East African/Eurasian-related sources.
3. Earliest modern human population divergence estimated 350–260 ka (outside V1 range; Phase 10).
