# Sümer et al. 2024 — Ranis and Zlatý kůň genomes

- **Citation:** Sümer AP, Rougier H, Villalba-Mouco V, et al. (2025) Earliest modern human genomes constrain timing of Neanderthal admixture. *Nature* 638:711–717 (online Dec 2024).
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-024-08420-x · **Verification:** full_text (open access)

## Claims we use
1. Individuals from Ilsenhöhle in Ranis (Germany, ~45 ka) and Zlatý kůň (Czechia) belong to one small, closely related population; they are the earliest split from the out-of-Africa population sampled so far and show no genetic continuity with later Europeans. *(Abstract; main text; discussion.)*
2. Their Neandertal ancestry derives from the same admixture event seen in all non-Africans, dated by them to 45–49 ka — later than or close to earlier estimates (52–57 ka, 47–65 ka, 41–54 ka). *(Main text, dating section.)*
3. Population size estimated ~300 (95% CI 242–374). *(ROH section.)*
4. The LRJ stone-tool technocomplex (central/NW Europe, ~41–47.5 ka) has been attributed to early modern humans. *(Introduction; via cited work.)*
