# Haak et al. 2015 — massive migration from the steppe

- **Citation:** Haak W, Lazaridis I, Patterson N, et al. (2015) Massive migration from the steppe was a source for Indo-European languages in Europe. *Nature* 522:207–211.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/nature14317 · PMC5048219 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. 69 Europeans, 8,000–3,000 years ago (390k capture). *(Abstract.)*
2. 8,000–7,000 years ago, closely related early farmers in Germany, Hungary and Spain differed from local hunter-gatherers; Russia was inhabited by hunter-gatherers (EHG) with high affinity to the ~24 ka Mal'ta individual. *(Abstract.)*
3. By 6,000–5,000 years ago, farmers across much of Europe had more hunter-gatherer ancestry than their predecessors (MN resurgence: ~18–34% more WHG-related ancestry than EN in Germany and Spain). *(Abstract; main text.)*
4. Yamnaya-associated steppe herders descended from EHG and from a population of Near Eastern ancestry. *(Abstract.)*
5. Late Neolithic Corded Ware-associated individuals from Germany traced ~75% of their ancestry to a Yamnaya-related source; modelled alternatively, turnover relative to Middle Neolithic estimated at 48–80%. *(Abstract; main text.)*
6. Steppe-related ancestry persisted in all sampled central Europeans to at least 3,000 years ago; Bell Beaker- and Únětice-associated groups had less Yamnaya-related ancestry than Corded Ware. *(Abstract; Discussion.)*

## Notes
- The paper's title links the migration to Indo-European languages. **We do not display language claims**: linguistic inference is beyond what ancient DNA shows directly, and the Indo-European homeland remains debated (see Lazaridis 2022/2025).
