# Wang et al. 2021 — formation of human populations in East Asia

- **Citation:** Wang CC, Yeh HY, Popov AN, et al. (2021) Genomic insights into the formation of human populations in East Asia. *Nature* 591:413–419.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-021-03336-2 · PMC7993749 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. 166 ancient East Asians (6000 BCE–1000 CE) and 46 present-day groups. *(Abstract.)*
2. Hunter-gatherers from Japan (Jōmon), the Amur River Basin, and Neolithic/Iron Age Taiwan and Tibetan-plateau people share a deeply splitting lineage, which the authors interpret as likely reflecting a Late Pleistocene coastal migration. *(Abstract.)*
3. Yellow River Basin farmer-related ancestry (~3000 BCE) contributed up to ~84% to some Tibetan groups and ~59–84% to Han Chinese. *(Abstract.)*
4. People of Taiwan (~1300 BCE–800 CE) derived ~75% of their ancestry from a lineage common in present-day Austronesian, Tai-Kadai and Austroasiatic speakers (likely related to Yangtze River farmers) and ~25% from a northern lineage related to, but distinct from, Yellow River farmers. *(Abstract.)*
5. Yamnaya-related steppe ancestry arrived in western Mongolia after ~3000 BCE, later displaced there while persisting in western China. *(Abstract.)*
