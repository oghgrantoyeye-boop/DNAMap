# Akbari et al. 2026 — Directional selection across West Eurasia

- **Citation:** Akbari A, Perry A, Barton AR, et al. (2026) Ancient DNA reveals pervasive directional selection across West Eurasia. *Nature* 654:419–428.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-026-10358-1 · PMC13189228 · **Verification:** full_text (read 2026-10-09, Europe PMC author manuscript)

Licence: CC BY (author manuscript released under HHMI's open-access policy). We still paraphrase and draw our own figures (CLAUDE.md).

## What the study is

A study of natural selection, not of population history. It tests each genetic variant for a steady rise or fall in frequency over time that population structure (who mixed with whom) cannot explain. It covers 15,836 ancient West Eurasians across about 18,000 years, 10,016 of them newly reported, plus 6,438 present-day people.

## Claims we use

1. The study finds 479 independent loci (410 outside the HLA region) with a probability of selection above 99% in roughly the last ten thousand years. That is about twenty times the yield of earlier ancient-DNA scans (12 loci in 2015, 21 in 2024). *(Abstract; "Hundreds of cases of directional selection"; introduction.)* → `ev-akbari2026-1`
2. **The multiple sclerosis risk allele HLA-DRB1\*15:01.**
   - Tagged by rs3135388, the allele was under positive selection, rising from about 0% to about 16% between roughly 6,000 and 2,000 years ago.
   - The authors argue that today's north–south difference in multiple sclerosis risk in Europe was not driven mainly by selection on the steppe: selection started south of the Caucasus in people without steppe ancestry.
   - After Yamnaya-related ancestry spread west, selection was stronger in northern Europe (s = 11.1 ± 2.5%) than in south-western Europe (6.1 ± 2.1%), and they identify this as the main driver of the north–south difference.
   - In the last ~2,000 years the allele was selected against.

   *("HLA-DRB1: Elevated MS risk in north Europe is not due to selection on the steppe", Fig. 3 panel 5; SI section 7.)* → `ev-akbari2026-2`. This contradicts `ev-barrie2024-1`; recorded as disagreement `d-ms-risk-steppe`.
3. The newly reported individuals' sites, coordinates and cultural context are deliberately withheld from this paper. The data are released for studies of selection; the contextual information will appear in later papers, which the authors say should be the references for population history and archaeology (an embargoed copy goes public in 2041). *(Data availability.)* → `ev-akbari2026-3`. This is why almost none of the 10,016 new individuals appear on our map. AADR v66.p1 credits this paper for 488 mapped individuals, all but one first published elsewhere.

## Read but not used (yet)

These findings are noted for a possible natural-selection feature. That feature needs the owner's decision (`research/notes/LOG.md`, 2026-10-09).

- **Single-variant stories with dated frequency changes:**
  - a celiac-disease risk allele (HLA-DQ2 tag) rose from ~0% to ~20% in the last 4,000 years;
  - blood group B rose from ~0% to ~10% over ~6,000 years while A fell;
  - the major tuberculosis risk allele at TYK2 rose from ~9,000 to ~3,000 years ago, then fell;
  - the hemochromatosis allele at HFE rose from ~5,000 to ~2,000 years ago, then levelled off and dipped;
  - CCR5-Δ32 rose from ~6,000 to ~2,000 years ago, too early for the medieval plague;
  - nine loci show selection for light skin, and none for dark skin;
  - the cystic fibrosis allele ΔF508 shows no directional selection.
- **Shifts in polygenic scores (combinations of many variants weighted by their effect in present-day studies):**
  - **Strong signals:** a rise in the light-pigmentation score; falls in scores for body fat and waist measures.
  - **Also reported:** falls in scores for bipolar disorder, schizophrenia and smoking, and rises in scores for intelligence-test results, household income and years of schooling.
  - **The authors' own caveats:**
    - these behavioural traits are defined only in present-day societies;
    - the same alleles overlap with those lowering type 2 diabetes-related traits;
    - selection on such scores has reversed in at least one recent population;
    - a signal does not mean the past target of selection was the trait measured today.
  - Any use of these findings must carry those caveats and the published critiques.
