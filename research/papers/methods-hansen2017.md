# Hansen et al. 2017 — DNA preservation in petrous bone and tooth cementum

- **Citation:** Hansen HB, Damgaard PB, Margaryan A, et al. (2017) Comparing ancient DNA preservation in petrous bone and tooth cementum. *PLoS ONE* 12:e0170940.
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pone.0170940 · PMC5271384 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. The inner petrous bone and the cementum layer of tooth roots are recognised as the best substrates for ancient human genomics. In paired samples from the same skulls (27 pairs), average endogenous DNA was 40.0% for petrous bone and 16.4% for tooth cementum; skull-vault (parietal) bone averaged about 2–3% (2.2% in the Abstract; 2.8% for nine skeletons in Results). *(Abstract; Introduction; Results: Endogenous DNA.)* [ev-hansen2017-1]
2. Teeth with good molecular preservation (>10% endogenous) matched their paired petrous bones on average, while poorly preserved teeth were clearly outperformed by petrous bone. *(Results; Fig 3; Discussion.)* [ev-hansen2017-2]
3. Endogenous content in ancient samples is often below 1% because DNA degrades over time and microbes invade skeletal tissue. *(Introduction.)* [ev-hansen2017-3]
4. Seven petrous bones from cremated skeletons had <0.03% endogenous DNA and showed no sign of authentic ancient human DNA. *(Results; Discussion: Cremated petrous bones.)* [ev-hansen2017-4]
5. Their protocol dissolved crushed samples in a digestion buffer of EDTA with proteinase K (24 h at 37 °C) and purified DNA by a silica-in-solution method with a binding buffer efficient for short fragments. *(Materials and Methods: DNA extraction, library preparation, and sequencing.)* [ev-hansen2017-5]
6. C-to-T mismatches at the first 5′ position ranged from 7% to 52% (average 26.5%) across 63 non-cremated extracts, as expected for authentic ancient DNA. *(Results: DNA deamination damage.)* [ev-hansen2017-6]
7. After discarding reads shorter than 30 bp, average lengths of mapped human reads were 37.0–77.1 bp in teeth (mean 54.5) and 39.2–65.0 bp in petrous bones (mean 52.9). *(Results: DNA fragment length.)* [ev-hansen2017-7]
8. Because shotgun sequencing reads molecules indiscriminately, the endogenous share is crucial for cost; petrous and cementum gave on average about 8 times the endogenous share of parietal bone, which the authors put as roughly an 8× genome for the price of a 1× genome. *(Introduction; Discussion: Endogenous DNA.)* [ev-hansen2017-8]
9. Reads were mapped to the human reference hg19 with BWA, duplicates removed, and endogenous content computed as mapped human reads divided by reads kept after trimming. *(Materials and Methods: Bioinformatics and DNA authenticity.)* [ev-hansen2017-9]
10. About 1.25 billion reads were generated from 76 libraries, about 16.4 million per library on average. *(Results: Endogenous DNA.)* [ev-hansen2017-10]
11. Mitochondrial-to-nuclear coverage ratios averaged 157 in well-preserved teeth and 79 in the paired petrous bones. *(Results: mtDNA and nuclear DNA.)* [ev-hansen2017-11]

## Notes
- The parietal average differs between Abstract (2.2%) and Results (2.8%, nine historical skeletons); we display "about 2–3%".
- One lab; samples from Central Asia (Bronze Age), England (Viking Age) and Denmark. Site effects were large.
