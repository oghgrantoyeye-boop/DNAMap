# Fu et al. 2016 — genetic history of Ice Age Europe

- **Citation:** Fu Q, Posth C, Hajdinjak M, et al. (2016) The genetic history of Ice Age Europe. *Nature* 534:200–205.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/nature17993 · PMC4943878 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. 51 Eurasians from ~45–7 ka: Neandertal ancestry fell from 3–6% in the earliest individuals to ~2%, consistent with selection against Neandertal variants. *(Abstract.)*
2. The earliest modern humans in Europe (e.g. Ust'-Ishim, Oase 1) did not contribute substantially to present-day Europeans; from ~37 ka (Kostenki 14) to ~14 ka, all sampled individuals descend from a single founder population that is part of present-day European ancestry. *(Abstract; "Genetic clustering" section.)*
3. Genetically defined clusters, named after type sites: Věstonice (Gravettian-associated, ~34–26 ka), Mal'ta (~24–17 ka, Siberia), El Mirón (Magdalenian-associated, ~19–14 ka; strong affinity to GoyetQ116-1), Villabruna (from ~14 ka). *(Clustering section; Fig. 2–3.)* Clusters were defined from genetic data first, and only then compared with archaeological cultures — an approach we follow.
4. From ~14 ka (Villabruna cluster) a component related to present-day Near Easterners appears in Europe; some Villabruna-cluster individuals share extra alleles with East Asians. *(Abstract; Villabruna section.)*

## Notes
- Cluster names are genetic labels, not peoples. Display them as "Villabruna-cluster ancestry" etc.
