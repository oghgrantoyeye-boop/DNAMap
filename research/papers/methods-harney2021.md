# Harney et al. 2021 — minimally destructive DNA extraction from teeth

- **Citation:** Harney É, Cheronet O, Fernandes DM, et al. (2021) A minimally destructive protocol for DNA extraction from ancient teeth. *Genome Research* 31:472–483.
- **DOI:** 10.1101/gr.267534.120 · PMC7919446 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. Standard ancient DNA sampling destroys material by drilling or cutting and powdering parts of bones and teeth, and concern about destructive sampling has grown with the field. *(Abstract; Introduction.)* [ev-harney2021-1]
2. Published protocols that reduce destruction of the petrous bone include sampling the inner-ear ossicles and targeted drilling of the cochlea through the cranial base of complete or reconstructed skulls. *(Introduction; describes Sirak et al. 2017, 2020, which we have not read.)* [ev-harney2021-2]
3. Their minimally destructive method wraps the tooth root in parafilm except a targeted lower part and soaks that part in an EDTA–proteinase K extraction buffer for 2.5 h at 37 °C; there is no drilling or grinding, and the tooth stays suitable for most morphological study and for radiocarbon dating. *(Abstract; Methods: DNA extraction; Discussion.)* [ev-harney2021-3]
4. In paired roots, the soaked root gave on average 35.8% endogenous molecules versus 18.8% for a powdered whole root, with no significant difference in library complexity. *(Results: Extraction efficiency; Fig 2.)* [ev-harney2021-4]
5. Petrous samples gave more complex DNA than cementum by one metric, and the authors say petrous sampling remains justified when preservation is poor; most teeth tested were moderately to very well preserved, and friable roots crumbled. *(Results; Discussion.)* [ev-harney2021-5]
6. Standard protocols remove the outer surface with a sanding disc or sandblaster; their method instead wiped the tooth with 2% bleach, rinsed with 95% ethanol and UV-irradiated it for 5–10 min per side, with contamination estimates similar to the destructive methods. *(Results: Contamination rate; Methods: Sampling.)* [ev-harney2021-6]
7. All work was done in dedicated clean rooms, and a negative control (extraction buffer in an empty tube) went through every extraction batch. *(Methods.)* [ev-harney2021-7]
8. About 50 mg of petrous powder was taken per bone; robotic extractions used about 37 mg of powder and manual ones up to 50 mg. *(Methods: Sampling; DNA extraction.)* [ev-harney2021-8]
9. Powders were incubated about 18 h at 37 °C in 0.45 M EDTA with proteinase K, then DNA was bound to silica columns following Dabney et al. 2013, or extracted robotically. *(Methods: DNA extraction.)* [ev-harney2021-9]
10. Libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NextSeq 500 with 2×76 or 2×101 cycles; adapters were trimmed and read pairs merged requiring a 15 bp overlap. *(Methods: Library preparation, enrichment, and sequencing; Bioinformatic processing.)* [ev-harney2021-10]
11. Merged reads were mapped to hg19 with BWA, chosen for consistency with most published ancient DNA; duplicates were defined as reads with the same start, end, orientation and barcode, keeping the best copy. *(Methods: Bioinformatic processing.)* [ev-harney2021-11]
12. Authenticity was assessed with mitochondrial match to consensus (contamMix), autosomal contamination from linkage disequilibrium (ContamLD), X-chromosome contamination in males (ANGSD), the terminal C-to-T rate and read lengths; authentic ancient DNA has very few reads longer than 100 bp. *(Results: Contamination rate; Methods: Bioinformatic processing.)* [ev-harney2021-12]
13. A minimum of 95% match to the mitochondrial consensus is typically applied when screening for population-genetic studies. *(Results: Contamination rate.)* [ev-harney2021-13]
14. Partial UDG (double-stranded) or USER (single-stranded) treatment removed damage inside molecules but kept it at the ends for authentication; libraries under 3% terminal C-to-T were treated as failing screening. *(Results; Methods.)* [ev-harney2021-14]

## Notes
- Thirty individuals; method tested mainly on well-preserved teeth. Not suited to pathogen work that targets the pulp.
