# Barrie et al. 2024 — Multiple sclerosis risk and steppe ancestry

## Claims we use

1. **The rise of the allele.** HLA-DRB1\*15:01 is the strongest single genetic risk factor for multiple sclerosis (odds ratio about 2.9). Its tag variant was first observed in a Neolithic individual from Italy and rose quickly around the emergence of the Yamnaya culture, about 5,300 years ago, in steppe and steppe-derived populations. *(Main text, Fig. 2b.)*
2. **The authors' interpretation.**
   - Selection on multiple sclerosis risk variants began among Pontic–Caspian steppe pastoralists.
   - It continued in the mixed populations of Europe after Yamnaya-related migration about 5,000 years ago.
   - The modern gradient in steppe ancestry, especially in the HLA region, may help explain the north–south gradient in multiple sclerosis prevalence, together with environmental factors.

   *(Abstract; Fig. 5; Discussion.)*
