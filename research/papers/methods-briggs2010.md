# Briggs et al. 2010 — removing deaminated cytosines with UDG

- **Citation:** Briggs AW, Stenzel U, Meyer M, et al. (2010) Removal of deaminated cytosines and detection of in vivo methylation in ancient DNA. *Nucleic Acids Research* 38:e87.
- **DOI:** 10.1093/nar/gkp1163 · PMC2847228 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. Most damage-derived errors in ancient DNA sequences come from hydrolytic deamination of cytosine to uracil, which appears as C-to-T (or, on the complementary strand, G-to-A) differences. *(Introduction.)* [ev-briggs2010-1]
2. Uracil–DNA glycosylase (UDG) removes uracils and leaves abasic sites; endonuclease VIII cuts the strand at those sites, and end repair then lets the shortened molecule still enter the library. *(Results: A UDG/endoVIII repair scheme; Fig 1.)* [ev-briggs2010-2]
3. In an untreated Neandertal library, C-to-T and G-to-A differences reached 40–50% at fragment ends; after UDG/endonuclease VIII treatment they were drastically reduced, and the mitochondrial error rate fell from 2.20% to 0.40% per base. *(Results: Repair of Neandertal DNA; Figs 4 and 6.)* [ev-briggs2010-3]
4. Reading both ends of each molecule and merging the pair (at least 11 overlapping bases) improves accuracy, because Illumina accuracy drops toward the end of each read; pairs that could not be merged were discarded. *(Results: Repair of Neandertal DNA; Neandertal DNA sequence accuracy.)* [ev-briggs2010-4]
5. Amplified libraries sequenced deeply contain several copies of each original molecule, which can be recognised by their orientation and start and end points. *(Results: Neandertal DNA sequence accuracy.)* [ev-briggs2010-5]
6. Without uracil removal, true C/T or G/A heterozygous sites are hard to tell from deamination at low coverage, which risks false heterozygote calls. *(Discussion.)* [ev-briggs2010-6]

## Notes
- Deaminated methylated cytosines (at CpG sites) become thymine and are not removed by UDG (Results); we do not display this detail.
