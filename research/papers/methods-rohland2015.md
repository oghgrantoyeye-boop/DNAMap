# Rohland et al. 2015 — partial UDG treatment and barcoded libraries for screening

- **Citation:** Rohland N, Harney E, Mallick S, Nordenfelt S, Reich D. (2015) Partial uracil–DNA–glycosylase treatment for screening of ancient DNA. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 370:20130624.
- **DOI:** 10.1098/rstb.2013.0624 · PMC4275898 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. DNA extraction and library preparation up to the set-up of the amplification step were carried out in a dedicated ancient DNA laboratory following established precautions. *(Material and methods (a): Clean room.)* [ev-rohland2015-1]
2. Ligating a unique pair of barcodes to each sample's DNA molecules removes undetected cross-contamination between samples from that step onward. *(Abstract; Summary.)* [ev-rohland2015-2]
3. Between 50 and 75 mg of bone powder was used per extraction, following Dabney et al. 2013; each sample has to be mechanically pulverised in clean conditions. *(Material and methods (c); Summary.)* [ev-rohland2015-3]
4. Production libraries are often built with UDG, which removes uracils (deaminated cytosines), and endonuclease VIII, which cuts at the resulting abasic sites, lowering ancient-DNA errors; untreated libraries remain useful for contaminated samples, where keeping only damaged molecules can greatly reduce contamination. *(Introduction.)* [ev-rohland2015-4]
5. Partial UDG treatment removes uracils inside molecules but keeps a substantial share at the terminal base: on average terminal damage fell about threefold (27% to 9%) and second-position damage about 26-fold, so one library can serve both to test authenticity and for population-genetic analysis. *(Abstract; Results (b); Table 1; Fig 3a.)* [ev-rohland2015-5]
6. The authors propose a terminal C-to-T rate of at least 3% as the authenticity threshold for partially UDG-treated libraries, scaling down the 10% threshold Sawyer et al. suggested for untreated libraries from samples over 500 years old; the samples come from one region and a 3,000–9,000-year range, so the threshold may need revision. *(Results (b).)* [ev-rohland2015-6]
7. Hybridisation capture is a standard tool because, for many ancient samples, much of the DNA in the library is not from the individual and only a subset of the genome is of interest. *(Introduction.)* [ev-rohland2015-7]
8. Sample indexes added by PCR let libraries and experiments be pooled for sequencing and separated afterwards by index. *(Introduction; Material and methods (j); Fig 1.)* [ev-rohland2015-8]
9. Libraries were sequenced paired-end (2×75 or 2×150 cycles) on a MiSeq; adapters were trimmed and read pairs merged requiring at least 15 bp of overlap. *(Material and methods (k).)* [ev-rohland2015-9]

## Notes
- Sixty bone samples from one region of Russia; we use only method claims.
