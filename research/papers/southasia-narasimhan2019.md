# Narasimhan et al. 2019 — formation of populations in South and Central Asia

- **Citation:** Narasimhan VM, Patterson N, Moorjani P, et al. (2019) The formation of human populations in South and Central Asia. *Science* 365:eaat7487.
- **DOI:** 10.1126/science.aat7487 · PMC6822619 · **Verification:** full_text (PMC author manuscript)

## Claims we use
1. 523 ancient individuals. The primary source of ancestry in South Asians is a prehistoric gradient between people related to early Iranian hunter-gatherers and people related to Southeast Asian / South Asian hunter-gatherers (AASI). *(Abstract; Fig. 5.)*
2. "Indus Periphery Cline": 11 outlier individuals at sites in Iran and Turkmenistan (Gonur, Shahr-i-Sokhta) in contact with the IVC; the authors flag that equating this cline with the IVC population is a hypothesis with circumstantial support. *(Main text.)*
3. After the IVC's decline, Indus Periphery-related people mixed with southeastern groups to form **Ancestral South Indians (ASI)** and with steppe-pastoralist-descended people (spreading via Central Asia after ~4000 BP) to form **Ancestral North Indians (ANI)**. *(Abstract; main text.)*
4. **AASI** ("Ancestral Ancient South Asians") is modelled as an Asian lineage that split around the time East Asian, Andamanese and Australian ancestors separated. *(Admixture-graph section; Fig. 5.)*
5. Steppe-related ancestry in South Asia matches that of Bronze Age eastern Europe (Middle–Late Bronze Age steppe groups). *(Abstract.)*

## Notes
- ANI, ASI and AASI are modelled ancestry components, not sampled populations — show as inferred, not as map points.
