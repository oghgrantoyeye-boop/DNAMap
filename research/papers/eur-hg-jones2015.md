# Jones et al. 2015 — Caucasus hunter-gatherers (CHG)

- **Citation:** Jones ER, Gonzalez-Fortes G, Connell S, et al. (2015) Upper Palaeolithic genomes reveal deep roots of modern Eurasians. *Nature Communications* 6:8912.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/ncomms9912 · PMC4660371 · **Verification:** full_text (open access)

## Claims we use
1. Genomes from western Georgia: Satsurblia (~13.3 ka, Late Upper Palaeolithic) and Kotias Klde (~9.7 ka, Mesolithic); plus Bichon (~13.7 ka, Switzerland). *(Abstract.)*
2. Caucasus hunter-gatherers form a distinct clade estimated to have split from WHG ~45 ka and from the ancestors of Neolithic farmers ~25 ka. *(Abstract.)* These split dates are model-based estimates.
3. CHG-related ancestry contributed significantly to Yamnaya-associated steppe herders. *(Abstract.)*
4. The authors suggest a possible link between CHG-related ancestry in South Asia and the arrival of Indo-Aryan languages — **flagged as speculative in the paper's own wording ("possibly"); we do not display it.**
