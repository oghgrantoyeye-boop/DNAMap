# Posth et al. 2018 (Nat Ecol Evol) — language continuity despite population replacement

- **Citation:** Posth C, Nägele K, Colleran H, et al. (2018) Language continuity despite population replacement in Remote Oceania. *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 2:731–740.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/s41559-018-0498-2 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. Direct evidence of a Papuan-related expansion into Remote Oceania beginning ~2,500 BP. *(Summary.)*
2. Subsequent near-complete replacement of Lapita/Austronesian-related ancestry by Near Oceanian ancestry in Vanuatu, through repeated migrations and sex-biased admixture with people from the Bismarck Archipelago. *(Summary.)*
3. Austronesian languages persisted despite this replacement — an example that genetic ancestry and language do not track each other. *(Summary.)*

## Disagreement / nuance with Lipson 2018
- Both papers agree on early Papuan-related arrival (~2500 vs by ~2300 BP); they differ in emphasis (single large movement vs incremental, repeated, sex-biased admixture). Store as two models.
