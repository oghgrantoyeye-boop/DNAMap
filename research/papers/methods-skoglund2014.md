# Skoglund et al. 2014 — separating ancient DNA from modern contamination by damage

- **Citation:** Skoglund P, Northoff BH, Shunkov MV, et al. (2014) Separating endogenous ancient DNA from modern day contamination in a Siberian Neandertal. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences USA* 111:2229–2234.
- **DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.1318934111 · PMC3926038 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. Despite strict laboratory precautions, many ancient human fossils already contain present-day human DNA from earlier handling. *(Significance; Introduction.)* [ev-skoglund2014-1]
2. The main diagnostic feature of ancient DNA is an excess of C-to-T differences rising toward the 5′ end of reads, usually with a complementary G-to-A excess at the 3′ end, attributed to cytosine deamination in single-stranded ends; unlike fragment length, it tends to increase over time. In samples several thousand years old under typical conditions, 30–40% of 5′-terminal Cs can read as T. *(Introduction; Results: Model Outline.)* [ev-skoglund2014-2]
3. Seeing this pattern shows that degraded DNA is present, but does not prove that modern contamination is absent or low. *(Introduction.)* [ev-skoglund2014-3]
4. Scoring each read for evidence of post-mortem damage (a PMD score) and keeping only high-scoring reads reduced simulated contamination of up to about 90–95% to negligible levels in their tests. *(Results: Artificial Contamination Experiments; Figs 2–3.)* [ev-skoglund2014-4]
5. In high-coverage data, contamination can show up as more alleles than a chromosome's copy number allows. *(Introduction.)* [ev-skoglund2014-5]
6. Contamination from a fossil's own microenvironment mainly adds statistical noise, whereas contamination from present-day humans can strongly bias population-genetic and phylogenetic analyses. *(Introduction.)* [ev-skoglund2014-6]

## Notes
- Rohland et al. 2015 (Introduction) note that libraries not treated with UDG are the ones suited to this kind of damage-based filtering.
- Briggs et al. 2007 had argued that damage could not efficiently separate endogenous from contaminating DNA; this paper and Sawyer et al. 2012 are later work that does use damage. We show both positions.
