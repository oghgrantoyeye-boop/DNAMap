# Dabney et al. 2013 — silica extraction that keeps ultrashort DNA

- **Citation:** Dabney J, Knapp M, Glocke I, et al. (2013) Complete mitochondrial genome sequence of a Middle Pleistocene cave bear reconstructed from ultrashort DNA fragments. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences USA* 110:15758–15763.
- **DOI:** 10.1073/pnas.1314445110 · PMC3785785 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. In the widely used earlier silica method, an extraction buffer of EDTA and proteinase K releases DNA from bone powder; DNA is then bound to silica with a binding buffer, washed with an ethanol buffer and eluted in a low-salt buffer. *(Results and Discussion: An Improved Ancient DNA Extraction Technique.)* [ev-dabney2013-1]
2. Tested with synthetic fragments, recovery by that method fell with length, from 72% at 150 bp to 22% at 35 bp. *(Results and Discussion; Fig 1.)* [ev-dabney2013-2]
3. A new binding buffer (guanidine hydrochloride, sodium acetate, isopropanol) used in larger volume with silica spin columns recovered fragments of 30 bp and longer efficiently; with single-stranded libraries, molecules as short as about 30 bp were recovered and sequenced. *(Introduction; Results and Discussion; Fig 2A.)* [ev-dabney2013-3]
4. The number of DNA fragments in an ancient sample is expected to rise steeply as length falls, so much of the information sits in very short molecules; published ancient libraries had been strikingly short of molecules under 40 bp. *(Abstract; Introduction.)* [ev-dabney2013-4]
5. Library methods add priming sites by attaching adapters to molecule ends, so even very short fragments can be sequenced in full, and damage patterns typical of ancient molecules can be measured to check authenticity. *(Introduction.)* [ev-dabney2013-5]
6. Commonly used double-stranded library methods include a size-selective clean-up after adapter ligation where short molecules are expected to be lost; the single-stranded method lacks this step and recovered more short sequences in direct comparison. *(Introduction.)* [ev-dabney2013-6]
7. DNA fragmentation is at least partly driven by depurination. *(Introduction.)* [ev-dabney2013-7]
8. Full-length molecule sequences were built from overlapping forward and reverse reads, unmerged reads were discarded, and duplicates were collapsed by calling a consensus of sequences with identical start and end coordinates. *(Materials and Methods: Enrichment, Sequencing, and Assembly.)* [ev-dabney2013-8]

## Notes
- The test specimen is a cave bear, not a human; we use only the method claims, which later human studies adopted (e.g. Rohland et al. 2015; Harney et al. 2021).
