# Fu et al. 2014 — Ust'-Ishim genome

- **Citation:** Fu Q, Li H, Moorjani P, et al. (2014) Genome sequence of a 45,000-year-old modern human from western Siberia. *Nature* 514:445–449.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/nature13810 · PMC4753769 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. Ust'-Ishim (western Siberia, ~45 ka) belonged to a population that lived before or around the time western and eastern Eurasian populations separated. *(Abstract.)*
2. His Neandertal ancestry (2.3 ± 0.3%) is similar to present-day Eurasians, but in longer segments, implying the Neandertal gene flow happened ~7,000–13,000 years before he lived (i.e. roughly 52–58 ka; the paper elsewhere gives ~50–60 ka). *(Abstract; main text.)*
3. He is not specially related to Onge (Andaman) versus East Asians, so a simple early-coastal / later-northern two-route model is not supported by this individual. *(Main text.)*

## Notes
- Ust'-Ishim is not known to have contributed detectably to later populations; it represents an early dispersal, not an ancestor of a specific later group.
