# Lazaridis et al. 2016 — origin of farming in the ancient Near East

- **Citation:** Lazaridis I, Nadel D, Rollefson G, et al. (2016) Genomic insights into the origin of farming in the ancient Near East. *Nature* 536:419–424.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/nature19310 · PMC5003663 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. 44 ancient Near Easterners, ~12,000–1,400 BCE (Natufian hunter-gatherers to Bronze Age farmers). *(Abstract.)*
2. The earliest Near Eastern populations derived about half their ancestry from a "Basal Eurasian" lineage with little or no Neandertal ancestry. *(Abstract.)*
3. The first farmers of the southern Levant (Israel/Jordan) and of the Zagros (Iran) were strongly differentiated and each descended largely from local hunter-gatherers. *(Abstract.)*
4. By the Bronze Age, Levant-, Zagros- and Anatolia-related farmer ancestries had mixed with each other (and with European hunter-gatherer-related ancestry), reducing differentiation. *(Abstract.)*
5. Directional summary by the authors: Anatolian-related farmer ancestry spread west into Europe; Levant-related farmer ancestry spread south into East Africa (East African ancestry better modelled by Levantine than Anatolian farmers); Iran-related ancestry reached the steppe; Iran-farmer- and steppe-related ancestry reached South Asia. *(Abstract; Extended Data Fig. 5.)*

## Notes
- Claim 5's South-Asia part is **contested** by Shinde 2019 / Narasimhan 2019 (Iranian-related ancestry in South Asia derives from a lineage that split before Zagros farmers; not a spread of western Iranian farmers). Store both.
