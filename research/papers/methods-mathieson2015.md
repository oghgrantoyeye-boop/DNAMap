# Mathieson et al. 2015 — the 1240k capture (methods only)

- **Citation:** Mathieson I, Lazaridis I, Rohland N, et al. (2015) Genome-wide patterns of selection in 230 ancient Eurasians. *Nature* 528:499–503.
- **DOI:** 10.1038/nature16152 · PMC4918750 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. The "1240k capture" uses in-solution hybridisation with synthesised probes to enrich 1,237,207 SNPs, combining a 394,577-SNP set (390k) and an 842,630-SNP set (840k); targets include SNPs on the X and Y chromosomes. *(Genome-wide ancient DNA from West Eurasia; Methods: Ancient DNA analysis.)* [ev-mathieson2015-1]
2. The authors state that the capture gives genome-wide data from samples with small fractions of human DNA and targets the sites that will be analysed; their median coverage on analysed SNPs was about 4 times higher than a large shotgun study, from 36 times fewer reads per sample. *(Genome-wide ancient DNA from West Eurasia; Extended Data Fig 1.)* [ev-mathieson2015-2]
3. Samples were removed if they had fewer than 30,000 targeted SNPs covered, mitochondrial evidence of contamination, X-chromosome heterozygosity despite being male, or an atypical X-to-Y ratio; sex was determined from the ratio of reads on the sex chromosomes. *(Methods: Ancient DNA analysis; Population history analysis.)* [ev-mathieson2015-3]
4. Ancient samples are represented by randomly sampled alleles rather than diploid genotypes, and statistics were computed with options that account for this. *(Methods: Population history analysis.)* [ev-mathieson2015-4]

## Notes
- We use this paper only for methods. Its population and selection results are not displayed on the extraction page.
