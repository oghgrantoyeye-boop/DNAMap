# Lipson et al. 2018 (Current Biology) — population turnover in Remote Oceania

- **Citation:** Lipson M, Skoglund P, Spriggs M, et al. (2018) Population turnover in Remote Oceania shortly after initial settlement. *Current Biology* 28:1157–1165.
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.cub.2018.02.051 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. Lapita-associated "First Remote Oceanians" (Vanuatu/Tonga ~2900–2600 BP) descend directly from the population that, from ~5000 BP, spread from Taiwan via the Philippines to western Melanesia, with minimal admixture with Papuan-ancestry populations on the way. *(Summary.)*
2. People of almost entirely Papuan-related ancestry arrived in Vanuatu by ~2300 BP; Papuan-related ancestry was later diluted but remains ≥80–90% on most islands today. *(Summary.)*
3. The Papuan-related ancestry in Vanuatu derives from the Bismarck Archipelago rather than nearer islands. *(Summary.)*
