# Mittnik et al. 2016 — genetic sex from the X-to-autosome read ratio (methods only)

- **Citation:** Mittnik A, Wang CC, Svoboda J, Krause J. (2016) A molecular approach to the sexing of the triple burial at the Upper Paleolithic site of Dolní Věstonice. *PLoS ONE* 11:e0163019.
- **DOI:** 10.1371/journal.pone.0163019 · PMC5051676 · **Verification:** full_text

## Claims we use
1. In most prehistoric skeletons, post-mortem decay leaves under 0.5% endogenous human DNA in most parts of the skeleton, the rest being mostly DNA from bacteria and fungi that colonised the body after death. *(Introduction; summarising earlier work.)* [ev-mittnik2016-1]
2. All work before amplification took place in clean rooms; bone was sampled with a sterile dentistry drill, 30–160 mg was used per extraction, negative extraction controls were included, and each library carried a sample-unique tag combination to guard against contamination from other libraries. *(Methods: DNA extraction and library preparation.)* [ev-mittnik2016-2]
3. Genetic sex can be assigned from Rx, the normalised ratio of reads on the X chromosome to reads on the autosomes: about 0.5 for males and 1.0 for females (male if the 95% interval is below 0.6, female if above 0.8). It worked down to about 1,000 reads, whereas the earlier Ry method (reads on Y versus X and Y) needs about 100,000 mapped reads. *(Introduction; Methods: Sex assignment; Discussion.)* [ev-mittnik2016-3]
4. Overlapping read pairs (11 or more bases) were merged before mapping to hg19. *(Methods: Sequencing and data analysis.)* [ev-mittnik2016-4]

## Notes
- The paper applies the method to a specific Upper Palaeolithic burial; we use only the method claims and display nothing about those individuals.
