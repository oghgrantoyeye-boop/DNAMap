## Founding population of Native Americans (inferred) (`first-americans-founders`)

*genetic cluster · confidence medium · Northeast Asia / Beringia (not located by genetics)*

A modelled population: the shared ancestors of Ancient Beringians and all other Native Americans, which separated from East Asian-related populations, received gene flow related to Ancient North Eurasians, and later branched. Its location during this time is not known from genetics.

**Membership:** inferred only (no sampled individuals).

**Evidence:**
- Demographic model: split from East Asians ~36 ± 1.5 ka with gene flow until ~25 ka; ANE-related gene flow 25–20 ka; Ancient Beringians branched 22–18.1 ka; northern and southern Native American branches diverged 17.5–14.6 ka. — morenomayar2018a, Abstract
- The ancestors of all present-day Native Americans entered the Americas from Siberia as a single migration wave no earlier than 23,000 years ago, after at most ~8,000 years of isolation in Beringia. — raghavan2015, Abstract
- An estimated 14–38% of Native American ancestry may derive from gene flow from a population related to MA-1, after divergence from East Asian ancestors and before Native American diversification. — raghavan2014, Abstract
- Native Americans diversified into two basal branches around 13,000 years ago. — raghavan2015, Abstract
- In a demographic model of Kolyma1, the Ancient Beringian USR1 and a present-day Karitiana genome, the ancestors of all three split about 30,000 years ago (95% CI 26.8-36.4) from East Asians, Kolyma1 split from the Ancient Beringian/Native American ancestors about 24,000 years ago, and ANS-related gene flow of about 18% (USR1) reached them about 20,000 years ago. — sikora2019, Results; Fig. 2; Supplementary Information 7
- Population structure between the northern (NA) and southern (SA) Native American lineages predates Anzick-1, which was on or near the SA lineage; the ANE-related gene flow into Native American ancestors happened before the NA and SA groups diverged. — rasmussen2014, Results
- The models suggest a refugium across southern Beringia during the Last Glacial Maximum; the authors describe a possible scenario in which early Ancient North Siberian-related groups met East Asian-related people there, and Ancient Beringians diverged from ancestral Native Americans in eastern Beringia, but offer an alternative. — sikora2019, Results; Extended Data Fig. 8
- Athabascans and Anzick-1 belong to the same initial migration wave as Amerindians; the authors think divergence of the Athabascan and Amerindian lineages was more likely in lower-latitude North America than in Alaska, a suggestion based on equidistant split times, the short interval before Anzick-1's age, and its location south of the ice sheets. — raghavan2015, Results; Discussion

**Caveats:**
- The window spans the modelled split from East Asians (~36,000 years ago) to the divergence of northern and southern branches (~14,600 years ago, lower bound) in Moreno-Mayar 2018 (abstract only); other studies place the founding migration later (no earlier than 23,000 years ago, Raghavan 2015).

## Anzick-1 (Clovis-associated) (`clovis-anzick`)

*genetic cluster · confidence high · Northern Rocky Mountains*

An infant buried with Clovis tools in Montana about 12,600 years ago, more closely related to all Indigenous Americans than to any other population.

**Archaeological context.** Clovis.

**Membership rule:** `^USA_Anzick_12700BP$`.

**Matched (1 individuals, 1 sites):** USA_Anzick_12700BP (1)

**Evidence:**
- Anzick-1, an infant buried with Clovis tools in Montana (~12,700–12,550 cal BP), is more closely related to all Indigenous American populations than to any other group. — rasmussen2014, Abstract
- The ANE (Mal'ta)-related gene flow into Native American ancestors is shared by Anzick-1, so it happened before about 12,600 years ago. — rasmussen2014, Abstract
- The Anzick-1 genome (male infant, bones dated 12,707-12,556 cal BP and directly associated with Clovis tools) was sequenced to 14.4x coverage; mitochondrial haplogroup D4h3a and Y haplogroup Q-L54*(xM3) were identified. — rasmussen2014, Results
- Anzick-1 shows closer affinity to all 52 Native American groups tested than to any present-day Eurasian population, less shared history with seven northern groups than with 44 Central and South American groups, and is compatible with belonging to a population directly ancestral to Karitiana and Maya; the authors say the Solutrean hypothesis for Clovis origins is not supported. — rasmussen2014, Results; Discussion
- Population structure between the northern (NA) and southern (SA) Native American lineages predates Anzick-1, which was on or near the SA lineage; the ANE-related gene flow into Native American ancestors happened before the NA and SA groups diverged. — rasmussen2014, Results
- Anzick-1 relatedness is greatest in Central and South Americans and lowest in North American groups; it is shared more by the ~10,900-year-old Los Rieles (Chile), ~9,600-year-old Lapa do Santo (Brazil) and later Central Andes individuals than by other South Americans. — posth2018, Results
- Two streams of North–South gene flow: one reaching the Central Andes by about 4,200 years ago, and one linking Anzick-1 to the oldest individuals from Chile, Brazil and Belize. — posth2018, Summary
- Later South Americans derive from lineages without specific Clovis affinity: a population replacement beginning at least 9,000 years ago, followed by substantial regional continuity. — posth2018, Summary
