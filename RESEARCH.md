# RESEARCH: synthesis for V1 (≈50,000 BCE – 1500 CE)

This is the project's reading of the ancient-DNA literature, written in our own words. Each statement points to a paper note in `research/papers/` (`topic-authorYEAR.md`), where the claim, its location in the paper, and our verification level are recorded. **Established** means shown by sampled ancient genomes in full-text-verified work, without published contradiction. **Debated** means published work disagrees or the authors hedge. **Not visualized** lists claims we deliberately leave out, and why.

Cross-cutting lessons, which shape the data model:
- **Culture ≠ genetic population.** Gravettian-associated individuals fall into two distinct genetic clusters (posth2023). Iberian and central European Beaker-associated individuals differ genetically (olalde2018). Distinct Pastoral Neolithic artefact traditions in East Africa show no genetic differentiation (prendergast2019). Dairy pastoralism in Bronze Age Mongolia cut across ancestry groups (jeong2020).
- **Language ≠ genetics.** Austronesian languages persisted in Vanuatu despite near-complete replacement of ancestry (posth2018). Many papers propose language links (Indo-European, Sino-Tibetan, Transeurasian, Celtic). **We do not display language claims** (see "Not visualized").
- **Proxy dependence.** Admixture proportions depend on the proxies chosen. Yayoi individuals fit both a Northeast Asian + Jōmon model and a Yellow River + Jōmon model (cooke2021). Yamnaya formation has several published models (below). Every proportion we show is labelled with its model and method.
- **Errata matter.** The headline claim of gallegollorente2015 (Eurasian ancestry reaching Yoruba and Mbuti) was withdrawn by erratum. Claims are checked against the current version of each paper.
- **Labels are not identities.** AADR group labels embed modern countries and sometimes later identities (e.g. `Belize_11700BP_Archaic_Maya`). We keep labels verbatim as data but never use them as population names (see `research/sources/aadr.md`).

## 1. Out of Africa, earliest Eurasians, archaic admixture

**Established**
- Present-day people outside Africa carry Neandertal-derived DNA from gene flow into the shared ancestors of non-Africans (green2010; prufer2014; prufer2017). Estimates: 1.5–2.1% (prufer2014) and 1.8–2.6% (prufer2017; East Asians 2.3–2.6%, western Eurasians 1.8–2.4%).
- The introgressing Neandertals were closer to the western Neandertals (Vindija, Mezmaiskaya) than to the Altai Neandertal (prufer2014; prufer2017).
- The earliest sampled modern humans in Europe and Siberia (~45 ka: Ust'-Ishim, Zlatý kůň/Ranis, Bacho Kiro; ~40 ka: Oase 1) had Neandertal segments longer than in present-day people. Several had Neandertal ancestors only a few generations back (fu2015; hajdinjak2021).
- Some of these early groups left no detectable descendants among later Europeans (Ust'-Ishim, Oase 1, Zlatý kůň/Ranis: fu2014; fu2015; fu2016; sumer2024). Bacho Kiro IUP individuals instead relate more to later East Asians and Native Americans (hajdinjak2021).
- From ~37 ka (Kostenki 14), sampled Europeans belong to a founder population that is part of later European ancestry (fu2016).
- Denisovan-related ancestry is highest in Oceania (reich2010; prufer2014) and especially among Ayta Magbukon in the Philippines (larena2021). East Asians carry Denisovan segments from at least two differently related sources (browning2018).

**Debated / model-dependent**
- **Date of the main Neandertal admixture.** 37–86 ka, most likely 47–65 ka (sankararaman2012); ~60 ka, 95% CI 55–84 (malaspinas2016); Ust'-Ishim implies ~7–13 kyr before ~45 ka (fu2014); 50.5–43.5 ka (iasi2024); 45–49 ka (sumer2024). The most recent estimates, which use early genomes, cluster at ~50–45 ka. We store all of them as alternative date models of one archaic admixture event.
- Whether there were one or several Neandertal pulses into the non-African ancestral population: iasi2024 attribute most ancestry to a single extended period.
- The number and timing of Denisovan admixture events (browning2018; larena2021). Proportions in Oceanians: 4–6% (reich2010), a first estimate that later work refined. We show it with `medium` confidence.
- Single vs multiple out-of-Africa dispersals for Australo-Papuans (malaspinas2016 favour a single origin once archaic introgression is modelled).

**Not visualized**
- Any route for the out-of-Africa dispersal, or any place where Neandertals and modern humans met. Genetics does not locate the encounter (sankararaman2012 suggest the Middle East only on archaeological grounds).
- Deep African divergence times (schlebusch2017: 350–260 ka). These are outside V1 and belong to Phase 10.

## 2. Late Pleistocene and early Holocene hunter-gatherer structure

**Established**
- Pre-LGM Europe: the Gravettian-associated **Věstonice cluster** (central and southern Europe) and **Fournol cluster** (western and southwestern Europe), related to ~35 ka Aurignacian-associated Goyet Q116-1 (posth2023).
- **Mal'ta (MA-1, ~24 ka)** and Afontova Gora 2 (~17 ka) in Siberia define **Ancient North Eurasian (ANE)** ancestry, basal to western Eurasians and related to Native Americans (raghavan2014).
- **Ancient North Siberians** (Yana, ~31 ka) are distantly related to early West Eurasian hunter-gatherers (sikora2019).
- Fournol-related ancestry persisted through the LGM in southwestern Europe (Solutrean- and then Magdalenian-associated individuals: the **GoyetQ2 / El Mirón cluster**) (fu2016; posth2023).
- From ~14 ka, **Villabruna/Oberkassel (WHG)** ancestry, linked to Epigravettian-associated people of Italy and the Balkans and with affinities to the Near East, spread across western and central Europe, largely replacing Magdalenian-associated ancestry (fu2016; posth2023).
- **EHG (Sidelkino cluster)** in eastern Europe has affinity to both WHG and ANE (posth2023; haak2015). There is a west–east cline among hunter-gatherers (mathieson2018) and a "great divide" from the Black Sea to the Baltic (allentoft2024).
- **Caucasus hunter-gatherers (CHG)**, ~13.3–9.7 ka, form a distinct clade (jones2015).
- **Natufians** (Levant, ~12,000–9,500 BCE) and early Near Easterners carry about half their ancestry from **"Basal Eurasian"** (lazaridis2016; Early European Farmers ~44% in lazaridis2014), a modelled lineage with no sampled individuals.
- **Taforalt** (Morocco, ~15 ka) relates to Natufians, with ~1/3 sub-Saharan African-related ancestry (vandeloosdrecht2018, abstract only).
- **Tianyuan** (~40 ka, China) is closer to Asians, but with an extra affinity to Goyet Q116-1 (yang2017).

**Debated / model-dependent**
- **Naming.** WHG ≡ Oberkassel cluster, and EHG ≡ Sidelkino cluster (renamed by posth2023). The Goyet Gravettian individuals are assigned differently by fu2016 (Věstonice-related) and posth2023 (intermediate). We show both names as aliases and flag the Goyet assignment as contested.
- **Split times** (CHG–WHG ~45 ka, CHG–Neolithic farmers ~25 ka (jones2015); Zagros–Anatolia 46–77 ka (broushaki2016)) are model-based. They are stored as `split` relationships with `medium` confidence and are not drawn as map events.

**Not visualized**
- Movement routes of any cluster. Only appearances at sampled sites and modelled relationships are shown.

## 3. Near Eastern farmers

**Established**
- Early farmers of the southern Levant and of the Zagros were strongly differentiated, and each descended largely from local hunter-gatherers (lazaridis2016; broushaki2016).
- Central Anatolian early farmers show ~80–90% continuity with the local ~15 ka hunter-gatherer (Pınarbaşı), plus Iranian/Caucasus- and Levant-related inputs (feldman2019).
- By the Chalcolithic and Bronze Age, these ancestries had mixed. Chalcolithic Peqi'in (Levant) is modelled as ~57% Levant Neolithic-, ~17% Iran Chalcolithic- and ~26% Anatolia Neolithic-related (harney2018).
- Anatolian-related farmer ancestry spread west into Europe, Levant-related farmer ancestry contributed to East Africa, and Iran-related ancestry reached the steppe (lazaridis2016; skoglund2017).

**Debated**
- **Iranian-related ancestry in South Asia.** lazaridis2016 describe Iran-farmer-related ancestry spreading east. shinde2019 and narasimhan2019 find that the Iranian-related ancestry in the Indus Periphery and IVC split *before* Zagros farmers, which argues against a spread of western Iranian farmers. Stored as a disagreement.

## 4. Farming spreads into Europe

**Established**
- Farming arrived in southeastern Europe in the mid-7th millennium BCE with migrants of Anatolian-related ancestry. The first farmers of northern and western Europe passed through southeastern Europe with limited hunter-gatherer admixture (mathieson2018; haak2015; lipson2017).
- Hunter-gatherer ancestry in farmers rose over time, at different rates in different regions: LBK ~4–5%, German Middle Neolithic ~17%, Iberian Middle Neolithic ~23%, Iberian Chalcolithic ~27% (lipson2017); Middle Neolithic ~18–34% more WHG-related ancestry than Early Neolithic (haak2015).
- Britain: farming arrived with continental farmers by 3975–3722 cal BCE. Their ancestry was ~74% Anatolian-farmer-related on average, with small amounts of WHG-related ancestry and no later hunter-gatherer resurgence (brace2019).
- West of the "great divide" there were large ancestry shifts with farming. To the east, there was no substantial shift in the same period (allentoft2024).

**Not visualized**
- Arrows tracing a "Danubian" or "Mediterranean" route. We show only sampled sites, the populations' appearance dates, and `ancestry` relationships. brace2019's Mediterranean-route inference is shown as an *ancestry affinity* (Iberian Neolithic → British Neolithic), not as a path.

## 5. Eneolithic steppe, Yamnaya, and steppe-related expansions

**Established**
- Eneolithic clines north of the Caucasus: **Caucasus–Lower Volga (CLV)**, **Volga** (e.g. Khvalynsk) and **Dnipro** (e.g. Serednii Stih) (lazaridis2025; nikitin2025).
- **Yamnaya-associated** individuals (~3300–2600 BCE) are genetically homogeneous across a vast range (lazaridis2025). Their ancestry combines CHG/Near-Eastern-related and EHG/eastern-European-hunter-gatherer-related components (haak2015; jones2015; wang2019; lazaridis2025).
- **Corded Ware-associated** individuals in Germany derive ~75% of their ancestry from a Yamnaya-related source (haak2015). Yamnaya-related ancestry reached most of Europe within ~1,000 years after ~5,000 BP (allentoft2024).
- **Britain:** Beaker-associated individuals carry high steppe-related ancestry, part of a ~90% replacement of the gene pool within a few centuries (olalde2018).
- **Iberia:** most Beaker-associated individuals lack steppe-related ancestry (8 of 32 had it) (olalde2018). By ~2000 BCE, ~40% of Iberian ancestry and nearly 100% of Y chromosomes derive from people with steppe-related ancestry, a strongly male-biased process (olalde2019).
- Steppe-related ancestry reached South Asia via Central Asia after ~2000 BCE, matching Middle–Late Bronze Age steppe groups (narasimhan2019).
- There is little Yamnaya-related ancestry in Bronze Age Anatolia (lazaridis2022; damgaard2018). Botai-associated horse herders were not closely related to the Yamnaya (damgaard2018).
- Middle–Late Bronze Age migrants from the continent contributed about half the ancestry of Iron Age England and Wales (patterson2022).

**Debated / model-dependent**
- **How Yamnaya-associated ancestry formed.** Competing models: EHG + a Near-Eastern-related population (haak2015); EHG + CHG/Iran-related (jones2015; lazaridis2016); a contribution from Middle Don hunter-gatherers (allentoft2024); CLV-related ~4/5 plus Ukraine Neolithic hunter-gatherer-related ancestry via Serednii Stih groups. Don Yamnaya, for example, are modelled as 79.4 ± 1.1% Core Yamnaya + 20.6 ± 1.1% UNHG (lazaridis2025). These are shown side by side as alternative `AdmixtureModel`s.
- **Interaction with Globular Amphora-associated farmers** before the westward expansion (allentoft2024). We display this as one paper's finding, with `medium` confidence.

**Not visualized**
- The Indo-European language homeland and spread (haak2015 title; lazaridis2022; lazaridis2025; narasimhan2019). These are linguistic interpretations layered on genetic results, and they are contested.
- Any notion of "invasion", violence, or social causes of male-biased admixture. The genetic signal (sex bias) is shown; explanations are not.

## 6. South Asia

**Established (with sparse sampling)**
- The **Indus Periphery Cline** (11 outliers at Gonur and Shahr-i-Sokhta) and the single IVC-context Rakhigarhi genome share a mixture of ancient-Iranian-related and AASI-related ancestry. They have little or no steppe-related ancestry (shinde2019; narasimhan2019).
- **ANI** and **ASI** are later modelled ancestral components: ASI formed as Indus Periphery-related people mixed with south-eastern (AASI-related) groups, and ANI with steppe-related ancestry added (narasimhan2019).

**Caveats:** the IVC is represented by **one** genome. The Indus Periphery individuals are outliers buried outside the IVC heartland. ANI, ASI and AASI are modelled components with no sampled unmixed members. All are flagged sparse or inferred.

## 7. East and Southeast Asia

**Established**
- A deep lineage links Jōmon-associated hunter-gatherers, Amur Basin hunter-gatherers, and Neolithic/Iron Age Taiwan and Tibetan-plateau people (wang2021). Jōmon IK002 is basal to East and Northeast Asian genomes examined (gakuhari2020).
- Northern China: genetic stability in the Amur region, and change over time in the Yellow River and West Liao River regions (ning2020). Northern East Asian ancestry spread into southern East Asia after the Neolithic (yang2020, abstract only).
- Taiwan (~1300 BCE–800 CE): ~75% ancestry from a southern lineage (likely Yangtze-farmer-related) and ~25% from a northern lineage (wang2021).
- Southeast Asia: early farmers (e.g. Man Bac, Vietnam) mix southern-Chinese-farmer-related and deeply diverged hunter-gatherer (Hòabìnhian-related) ancestry, with further influxes by the Bronze Age (lipson2018sea; mccoll2018 abstract only).
- Japan: Jōmon-related, then Northeast-Asian-related (Yayoi period), then East-Asian-related (Kofun period) ancestry (cooke2021).
- Mongolia: a pastoralist expansion ~3000 BCE; three structured Late Bronze Age groups that all practised dairy pastoralism; Xiongnu-period mixing; higher eastern Eurasian ancestry in the Mongol period (jeong2020).

**Not visualized**
- Language-family spread claims (Sino-Tibetan, Austroasiatic, Transeurasian, Tocharian) in wang2021, lipson2018sea and robbeets2021.

## 8. Peopling of the Americas

**Established**
- Native American ancestors derive from a population that split from East Asians and received ANE-related gene flow (raghavan2014; rasmussen2014; morenomayar2018a). Anzick-1 (Clovis-associated, ~12.6 ka cal BP) is closest to Indigenous Americans (rasmussen2014).
- **Ancient Beringians** (USR1, ~11.5 ka) are basal to all other Native Americans (morenomayar2018a, abstract only).
- Two basal branches diverged (~17.5–14.6 ka per morenomayar2018a; ~13 ka per raghavan2015).
- South and Central America: rapid radiation from one branch; an early stream with Clovis affinity was followed by replacement from ≥9 ka and then regional continuity; gene flow reached the Central Andes by ~4.2 ka (posth2018). Andean structure was in place by ~5.8 ka (nakatsuka2020).
- Caribbean: Archaic-age people derive from a population closest to Central and northern South Americans. They were >98% replaced by Ceramic-age people related to northeastern South American Arawak speakers, who reached the Greater Antilles ≥1.7 ka (fernandes2021).

**Debated**
- **Timing of entry.** "No earlier than 23 ka" for the founding migration wave (raghavan2015) vs. demographic models with a split from East Asians ~36 ka and Beringian structure (morenomayar2018a), vs. archaeological footprints at White Sands dated ~23–21 ka (bennett2021; its dating has been debated, and we have not yet read the critiques). Represented as a disagreement with `medium` confidence on all positions.
- **"Population Y" / Australasian-related signal** in some Amazonian groups (skoglund2015; morenomayar2018b). The source population is unsampled, so the signal is shown as `low` confidence and inferred.

**Not visualized**
- Entry routes (coastal vs ice-free corridor). Neither is settled by genetics.
- Any link to the "Solutrean hypothesis". rasmussen2014 explicitly finds Anzick-1 related to Native Americans. We mention it only on the About page if at all.

## 9. Oceania and the Austronesian-associated expansion

**Established**
- Lapita-associated first Remote Oceanians (Vanuatu ~3000 BP; Tonga ~2700–2300 BP) had little or no Papuan-related ancestry. They descend from the population that spread from Taiwan via the Philippines (skoglund2016; lipson2018oceania).
- Papuan-related ancestry arrived in Vanuatu by ~2500–2300 BP, with near-complete replacement and sex-biased admixture, and derives from the Bismarck Archipelago (lipson2018oceania; posth2018oceania). Present-day South Pacific groups carry ≥25% Papuan-related ancestry (skoglund2016).
- Polynesian settlement sequence inferred from present-day genomes: from Samoa through to Rapa Nui (~1200 CE) (ioannidis2021). `medium` confidence (present-day data, model dates).

**Debated**
- One large movement vs incremental, repeated movements in the Papuan-related arrival (lipson2018oceania vs posth2018oceania emphasis).

**Not visualized**
- Voyaging routes between islands. ioannidis2021's sequence is shown as dated relationships between island groups, not as tracks.

## 10. Africa: forager structure, herders, and the spread of farming

**Established**
- Deep forager structure: three highly divergent sources (eastern, southern, and a widespread component highest today in central African rainforest hunter-gatherers), probably formed 80–20 ka and stable thereafter (lipson2022). San-related ancestry was once widespread: ~2/3 in Malawi hunter-gatherers (8.1–2.5 ka) and ~1/3 in Tanzanian hunter-gatherers (~1.4 ka) (skoglund2017).
- Shum Laka (Cameroon, ~8 and ~3 ka): ancestry most similar to west-central African hunter-gatherers. Present-day Bantu speakers do not descend substantially from this population (lipson2020).
- East African pastoralism spread in several steps: northeastern-African-related ancestry mixed with eastern African foragers to form Pastoral Neolithic groups, with further admixture by the Iron Age (prendergast2019; wang2020). The Luxmanda pastoralist (~3.1 ka) is modelled with 38 ± 1% Levantine-PPN-related ancestry (skoglund2017).
- Western-African-related (Bantu-language-associated) ancestry spread with farming. There was complete replacement of hunter-gatherers in some regions (skoglund2017), a serial-founder pattern, and local gene flow along the way (forteslima2024; patin2017 abstract only).
- Medieval Swahili coast (1250–1800 CE): African (mainly female-line) and Asian (mainly Persian-related male-line) ancestry, mixing from ~1000 CE (brielle2023).

**Debated**
- The Levant-related ancestry in East African pastoralists: migration from the Levant vs. a shared earlier ancestral population (skoglund2017 offer both).

**Withdrawn**
- gallegollorente2015's claim of Eurasian ancestry in Yoruba and Mbuti (erratum 2016).

## Regions and periods with too few samples (flag as sparse)
From the AADR inspection: before 15,000 BP there are only ~150 individuals worldwide. Africa south of the Sahara, South Asia (one IVC genome), Australia and New Guinea (a handful of late individuals), Southeast Asia before 4,000 BP, and most of the Americas before 8,000 BP have very few samples. Europe after 8,000 BP is heavily oversampled relative to everywhere else. The UI must show these gaps (sample-density strip, sparse badges), not hide them.

## What we will not visualize in V1 (summary)
1. Language spread or homeland claims.
2. Migration routes or paths between sampled places (no curved trajectories, no particles).
3. Social or causal explanations (conquest, violence, epidemics) beyond what the genetics shows.
4. Polygenic scores or "trait" reconstructions.
5. Links from ancient individuals to present-day ethnic or national groups (Phase 9 only, via supported ancestry relationships).
6. Claims only available from non-checked sources (e.g. Reich 2018 book text), or retracted/erratum-affected claims.
